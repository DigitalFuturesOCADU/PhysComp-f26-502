Workshop Goal

Connect the light sensor to the servo so that light controls movement. Explore three ways to link them: continuous mapping with map(), threshold-triggered oscillation, and threshold-triggered timed moves. Throughout the workshop, keep something attached to the servo horn — it makes the motion readable.

Activity

All workshops in Project 3 have two components: a working prototype and a design sketch. Over the three workshops you will develop both.

Part 1 — Working Prototype: Light controls the servo

Follow the Light Sensor to Servo — Step-by-Step Guide from beginning to end.

  1. Wire the photoresistor and confirm it sends values to the Serial Monitor.
  2. Add the servo and confirm it moves to a fixed angle.
  3. Use map() to connect the two — light level controls servo position continuously.
  4. Replace map() with a threshold: switch between two different oscillate() profiles based on light level.
  5. Swap oscillate() for moveServoA(): send the servo to different target angles when light crosses the threshold.
  6. Have something attached to the servo horn throughout — a pipe cleaner, folded paper, or wire — so the motion is visible.

Going further

Once the core walkthrough is working, pick whichever guide is most relevant to your design direction:

  • Multiple Servos — add a second servo and run both independently
  • Multiple Light Sensors — add a second photoresistor and compare readings for directional sensing
  • Two LDRs + Two Servos — combinatory patterns for the full 2+2 setup
  • Physical Adjustments — extending wires to mount sensors and servos away from the breadboard

Part 2 — Project Design V2

Now that you have experienced the sensor controlling the servo, revise your design concept. Read the project brief again. V2 can develop V1 with more detail and specificity, or it can be a new direction entirely. The drawing should show:

  1. Which sensing method you plan to use — continuous mapping, threshold switching, or comparison between two sensors.
  2. Position and orientation of the sensor — what specific light condition will it be reading?
  3. What the servo is attached to, and what that attachment does when it moves.
  4. Overall form factor and scale — on a table, free-standing, hanging, mounted on a wall, etc.
  5. How V2 develops or differs from V1 — what changed after working with the hardware?

Tip: You have now felt the difference between map() (fluid, continuous response) and a threshold (a sharp state change). One of those qualities is likely a better fit for your concept — let that inform the sketch.
